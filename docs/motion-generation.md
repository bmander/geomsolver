## The idea

In the plane the envelope condition `n · v = 0` is a statement about geometry. Every relative
velocity of two plane bodies is a turn about their instant centre, so the tool's normal at the
cutting point passes through it: the law of gearing. So a generated profile is a curve with one
scalar unknown per roll. The unknown is the tool's own parameter `s`, held by
`F(s, t) = X_s × X_t = 0`, which also covers a pure translation, whose instant centre is at
infinity. It is the trace machinery's bargain: a curve the solve sees, written over the geometry
that generates it. The tool's coordinates and the motion's centres and lines are its columns, so a
contact on the cut moves them, and a conjugate is synthesised, not stated.

A hand-written trace says the same thing: `tests/generation.rs::RackFlank` is a component whose
statements put the cut point at the foot of the normal from the pitch point. The construct and the
trace agree to 1e-9. The construct exists because a trace cannot hold a contact against a curve
tool, a spline cam or another generated tooth, and because it shares its words with the 3D
`envelope` and `motion`.

## Exactness

A residual is never a difference (spec §6.5's rule: a curvature by difference solves to a slightly
wrong circle and calls it right).

- **Traces.** `C''` and `C'''` are the implicit function's Taylor orders: put the truncated path
  through every row in Taylor arithmetic, read the `ε^k` coefficient with `q_k` unknown, and solve
  `Jq q_k = −R_k` with the factorisation `C'` already paid for. This is Wagner, Walther & Schaefer,
  "On the efficient computation of high-order derivatives for implicitly defined functions"
  (CPC 181, 2010). The rows' kernels are read over `taylor::Jet`: affine ones by their Jacobian,
  23 nonlinear ones written out. `tests/taylor.rs` holds each form to its kernel along random
  cubic paths.
- **Generated profiles.** The root `s(ε)` is found order by order, each order one division by
  `F_s`. A point, line, circle or arc is exact at every order, so a profile it cuts is exact to
  `C'''`. A formula-curve tool stops at its tape's third derivative, so its profile is exact to
  `C''`. That is also why a profile can cut in turn, read as its Taylor series: a rack-cut tooth is
  exact to the third order, so its mate is exact to the second.
- **Jacobians only.** `C'''` where it is not exact, and every gradient along the columns, are
  differences. The column gradients are central, because a claim is judged by the rank of the
  Jacobian they sit in: forward differences made `generated_spur.sv`'s theorem read as
  independent.

## Demonstrations (the issue's list)

- **The Jansen foot path tangent to the ground, with link lengths solved.** The frame's pivot or
  the toe rod solves so the stride touches a fixed ground. Stood on the ground at both of its dips
  (double support, a flat stride), the toe rod solves to 65.684, where Jansen's number is 65.7. A
  circle of radius 150 osculating the stride at a pinned crank angle also solves the rod.
- **A conjugate cam pair.** A lobe (a formula curve) turning about one centre cuts its conjugate
  turning about the other. At every roll the two touch with their common normal through the pitch
  point.
- **A rack-generated spur tooth contacting its mate.** The tooth is the envelope of a rack flank;
  the mate is the envelope of the tooth. The mate comes out the involute of the gear's base circle.
  At the mesh the two radii of curvature add to the line of action, which is Euler–Savary for
  involutes. In `generated_spur.sv`, the claim that the mate's centre of curvature lies on the base
  circle is judged a theorem.
- **Unification with 3D.** A turn about a point reads in space as the rotation about the line
  square to its view (`Family::read`). The rack's tooth swept as a solid under the same motion has
  the 2D envelope as its boundary: the material field changes sign across it.

## Prior art (scan of 2026-10-03; abstracts and catalogue pages, not full texts)

- **Higher-order implicit derivatives:**
  - Wagner, Walther & Schaefer, CPC 2010.
  - Griewank, Utke & Walther, Math. Comp. 2000: mixed partials by univariate Taylor sweeps.
  - Griewank & Walther, *Evaluating Derivatives*, 2008.
  - Fike & Alonso, hyper-dual numbers, AIAA 2011.
  - Multibody acceleration analysis: Haug 1989, Nikravesh 1988.
- **Curvature synthesis is per-mechanism or by hand:**
  - Kinzel, Schmiedeler & Pennock, JMD 2006/2007, and Schmiedeler et al., DETC 2011:
    geometric constraint programming with hand-built Bobillier constructions, four-bars.
  - Baskar & Plecnik, ARK 2020: higher-order four-bar synthesis by homotopy.
  - Cera et al., MMT 2022: four-bars and swinging blocks.
- **Envelopes and conjugates:**
  - Litvin & Fuentes 2004: the equation of meshing, curvature relations, local synthesis.
  - Innocenti, DETC 1992: planar conjugates.
  - Vogel & Griewank: hypoid flank curvatures exact by automatic differentiation.
  - KISSsoft's tool envelopes.
- **Dynamic geometry:**
  - GeoGebra's algebraic `Envelope` (Botana & Abánades 2013; Botana & Kovács 2016).
  - Geometry Expressions' loci (Todd, ADG 2006), which need the generating figure fixed.
  - Neither solves back through an envelope.
- **Not verified:**
  - whether D-Cubed allows G2 constraints against evaluated parametrics;
  - Geometry Expressions' full manual.

What looks new is the packaging rather than any algorithm. A point, tangent or curvature contact
works against the locus of any user-written sub-figure, and against the envelope of any tool under
a planar motion, each solved in the same solve as the geometry that generates it.

## Open

- **Shared contact parameters.** A tangency and a curvature at the same place on one curve are two
  contacts with two parameters. Tying them through other geometry is a degenerate root (the Jansen
  test pins the angle instead). A contact parameter that can be named, or a joint tangent-and-
  curvature contact, would say it regularly.
- **Singular points attract contacts.** At an involute's cusp `C' = 0`, every line through it is
  "tangent", and a tangency seeded nearby can land there (`tests/generation.rs`'s tangency test
  seeds clear of it). A contact refusing `|C'| ≈ 0` would turn the wrong answer into a refusal.
- **`locus::sweep` ignores a failed Newton step**, so a trace swept from a singular home draws one
  repeated point. `curve_closed` now refuses a degenerate polyline; the sweep itself still draws
  nothing useful.
- **Tools not yet:**
  - traced curves and splines (a spline cam is the common case; its basis gives three derivatives,
    so a spline tool is exact to `C''`);
  - generated profiles whose own tool is a curve.
- **3D.** The generating-sweep admission (M1) still refuses a translation, so a rack cannot cut a
  solid gear for native export, though the material field reads it.
