# Generation by motion

Curves and solids that nobody draws: each is what a tool leaves behind as it moves, or the path a
point of a mechanism traces. The solver treats each one as a curve of the drawing, so you can
constrain against it (put a point on it, hold a line tangent to it, fit a circle to its
curvature) and solve for the geometry that generates it. Issue #61, proposal 2;
[docs/motion-generation.md](../../../docs/motion-generation.md) explains how it works.

Read them in this order:

| File | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `stride.sv` | **Solving through a traced path.** A Jansen leg, its crank driven by `param t`, has its toe path held tangent to level ground at both of its dips, and the toe rod's length solves (65.684; Jansen's is 65.7). |
| `osculating_stride.sv` | **Two contacts at one place.** The same leg touches level ground once, at the stride's bottom, and a circle of radius 150 osculates the stride there. Both contacts are pinned to one unknown place (`t == s`), and the toe rod solves (66.869). |
| `dwell.sv` | **Exact curvature of a traced path.** A circle osculates a four-bar's coupler curve where the curve is nearly circular, and the coupler point's offset solves so the dwell arc has the radius you state. |
| `heart_cam.sv` | **A cam generated by its follower.** A roller rises and falls at uniform speed, driven by one law of the cam's turn `t`, and the cam is the envelope of the roller, one half per half turn, each reading the law on its own side of its corner. A circle reads the profile's curvature. |
| `dwell_cam.sv` | **A cam with dwells.** The follower rests, rises 12 on the cycloidal law, rests and returns, all one law of `t` joined with `min` and `max`. Each of the four stretches is its own envelope, the dwells arcs about the cam's centre. |
| `rocker_cam.sv` | **A driven turn.** The follower is an arm swinging about a pivot by the simple harmonic law (`arm spins(1) pivot` and `fix(value == …) arm`), its roller on the pivot's arc, and the cam is cut from the swing. |
| `flat_cam.sv` | **A flat-faced follower.** The tool is a line, the follower's face. The cam is its support function `base + s`, touching the face at `(p, -p')` in the follower's frame and bending with radius `base + s + s''`, which a circle held osculating it reads (29.09). |
| `sized_cam.sv` | **A cam sized against its curvature.** The base circle is an unknown. A circle of the least radius allowed, 15, is held osculating the rise where `s + s''` is least (`cos(360deg x) = -1/8`), and the solve answers the base through the law (21.398). |
| `cam_plate.sv` | **A cam plate as a solid.** The plate is a blank less the tappet swept under its law. Its rising flank is the tappet face's side generated under the drive, and a gauge drawn square to the page is held on it. |
| `conjugate_cams.sv` | **A partner generated by its mate.** An off-centre elliptical lobe turning on one shaft cuts the cam it drives on another. Their common normal always passes through the pitch point (the law of gearing, which nothing states). |
| `pin_wheel.sv` | **Clock gearing.** A lantern pinion's pin generates the wheel's tooth flank: the epicycloid its centre runs, set in by its radius. |
| `spur_mesh.sv` | **Generation twice over.** A rack cuts a tooth, and the tooth cuts its mate (an involute). The claim that the mate's centre of curvature lies on its base circle is judged a theorem. |
| `rack_cut_gear.sv` | **The same motion in 2D and 3D.** A rack cuts a 20-tooth spur gear out of a solid blank. `solventc --step --stl` exports it exactly, one sector patterned into the whole. The same motion draws the involute and root fillet as curves on its face. |
| `gauged_gear.sv` | **A contact in space on a generated surface.** The same gear with its rack's pressure angle left free. A gauge drawn in a view square to the page is held to the flank surface the rack's side cuts, and the solve answers the angle (20°). The flank is the tooth's involute extruded through the gear, and the export builds it that way too (issue #70). |

What the words are:

- A motion is a frame's joint: `m := frame` declares the frame, `m spins(r) o` turns it about a
  point `o` in the view `o` is drawn in, `m turns(r) ax` about a line, and `m slides(d) l` slides
  it along a line, `d` a turn (`use std (spins, turns, slides)`).
- `envelope(tool over t in (a, b), under: m)` is what a point, line, circle, arc, formula curve or
  another envelope cuts. `seen_from: n` reads it as `m` seen from the frame `n`
  (`under: rack, seen_from: blank`). `side: near | far` picks a circle's side.
- `c tangent(t == s) l` pins a contact's place to an unknown, `param s: Angle hint(318)`: every
  contact on the same curve pinned to `s` shares that one place, seeded where `s` is declared.
- `param t: Angle over (0deg, 360deg)` declares the crank's angle as a number that runs, and
  `trace(leg.toe over t)` is where the drawing's own toe goes as it does (`Leg(…, theta: t)`).
  The drawing is drawn at `t = 0` and followed round. A number the drawing leaves unbound
  (`leg.h`, `bar.ap`) does not run with `t`, and is an unknown the solve answers for through the
  path. (`p over theta in (a, b)` traces a point of a component as one of its numbers runs.)
- `envelope(surface(prism, edge: e), under: m, …)` is the surface a prism's side cuts. When `m`
  keeps the prism's view, that surface is `e`'s envelope extruded, and `p on` it holds a point drawn
  in any view.
- `sweep(tool over t in (a, b), under: m)` is the solid a tool sweeps through, and `cut` takes
  it out of a body.

- `fix(value == law) f` drives a joint by a law of the declared `t`: `f` stands at the law's value
  of the roll, so `envelope(… under: f, seen_from: cam)` is a cam cut from the law. A slide's
  value is an angle, so each cam says what a turn of it is (`slides(h)`: `360deg` is the whole
  lift). A law with corners (`min`, `max`, `abs`) takes one envelope per piece, each reading the
  law on its own side.

The gears and `conjugate_cams.sv` stay on constant-rate joints: their partners roll at a fixed
ratio, so there is no law to state. Only the cams write a law, and `heart_cam.sv` was the last
document to stand two constant-rate slides in for one.

Each file's test is in `rust/gcs-core/tests/generation_examples.rs` (with `generation.rs` and
`rack_cut.rs`), and the cams after `heart_cam.sv` in `cams.rs`, each against a closed form or
arithmetic of its own.
