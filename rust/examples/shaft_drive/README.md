# A shaft-drive bicycle transmission

A chainless drive for a bicycle. The shaft is sealed inside the drive-side chainstay. A step-up
gearbox sits inside an oversized bottom bracket and a step-down gearbox inside the rear dropout.
It is written in Solvent and checked with the **released `solventc` v0.1.18**. It is also a
benchmark: a hard target for the language and its kernel. It was first drawn against v0.1.17,
and its findings were filed as [#904](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/issues/904).
[What solventc lacks](#what-solventc-lacks) says which of them v0.1.18 fixed, and what is still
open.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `dims.sv` | the design table: duty, tooth counts, ratios, stresses and wind-up, with the claims that check them |
| `gears.sv` | `Helical` (an external helical gear generated by a helical rack) and `Ring` (an internal helical gear shaped by an external one) |
| `planetary.sv` | the bottom bracket's helical planetary: sun with its sleeve, three planets on pins, the shaped ring and the carrier |
| `mesh.sv` | the planetary's meshes checked as they roll: sun, planet and ring turned at their ratios over a sun pitch |
| `drive.sv` | the assembly: frame datums, the hypoid pairs, the planetary, the shaft, the frame (case, pinion housings, chainstay, dropout case), the hub, the spindle and the cranks |

```
solventc shaft_drive/dims.sv                        # the design table, every claim judged
solventc shaft_drive/mesh.sv                        # the planetary's meshes, rolled over a sun pitch
solventc shaft_drive/planetary.sv --png planetary.png --view top --hide box.carrier
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv                       # solve, diagnose, judge the clearances
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png drive.png --view 300,25
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png cut.png --view 290,30 --section 'y=-40mm'
```

Run these from `rust/examples/`. The drive imports `spiral_bevel/hypoid.sv`, and modules are
looked up from the opened file's directory and its ancestors.

## The design

**Why the shaft spins fast.** A drive shaft is a torsion spring between the cranks and the wheel.
Under each pedal stroke it winds up, then gives the energy back late and out of phase. That
springiness is what makes shaft bikes feel soft. The wind-up is `T·L/(G·J)`, and the torque the
shaft carries is the crank torque divided by how much faster than the cranks the shaft turns.

So this drive steps the speed up by `k = 6` before the shaft and back down by 2 at the hub. The
shaft then carries a sixth of the crank torque and winds up a sixth as far. That angle is divided
by 6 again on its way back to the crank. The rider feels a spring `k² = 36` times stiffer than the
same shaft driven 1:1. The 16 × 1.5 mm steel shaft winds up 0.42° at the cranks in a 180 N·m
sprint, against 15.3° if it were driven directly (`dims.sv`: `wind`, `wind_direct`).

**The train.**

| stage | where | gears | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | inside the bottom bracket | helical planetary: spindle → carrier, ring held in the shell, sun out. Sun 30 (right hand), three planets 15 (left), ring 60 (left); normal module 1 mm, 20° normal pressure angle, 20° helix, 14 mm face | × 3 |
| 2 | inside the bottom bracket | hypoid gear 48 on the sun's sleeve drives the hypoid pinion 24 on the shaft; mean module 1.25 mm, 25° spiral, 15.6 mm offset | × 2 |
| 3 | inside the rear dropout | the same hypoid pair turned end for end: the shaft's pinion 24 drives the hub's gear 48 | ÷ 2 |

- **Overall:** the wheel turns 3 times as fast as the cranks, like a 48 × 16 single speed. At
  90 rpm that is 35 km/h on a 700 × 35C tyre. The shaft turns at 540 rpm and carries 8.3 N·m
  cruising (50 N·m at the crank) or 30 N·m sprinting.
- **Quiet and efficient.** Every gear is helical or spiral. Every mesh has more than one tooth
  pair across the face (overlap ratio 1.52) as well as round the profile (transverse contact
  ratio 1.19 sun–planet, 1.36 planet–ring). The hypoids' face mill has its pressure shift
  balanced, as the library pair's is: found where its two flanks nearest undercut stand equally
  far from it. In the planetary, a planet's axial thrust from the sun and from the ring
  cancel, so the planets run free of axial load.
- **Stresses.** These are estimates, to be checked to ISO 6336 before tooling. At the sprint
  peak the planet's Lewis root stress is 289 MPa, against 450 MPa allowable for case-hardened
  18CrNiMo7-6. The shaft's shear is 66 MPa and the spindle's 151 MPa. Each is a claim in
  `dims.sv`.
- **Packaging.**
  - The bottom bracket is an 82 mm gearbox shell, 123 mm face to face. Its right end closes
    round the right-hand spindle bearing, outboard of the hypoid pinion. The pinion's housing
    is a 56 mm cylinder about the shaft's axis, round a 46.8 mm pinion, that runs back into
    the 30 × 2 mm chainstay.
  - The shaft lies in a plane 40 mm out from the frame's mid-plane on the drive side. At the hub
    that clears the spokes of a flange at −11 mm.
  - The dropout's gear case is an 80 mm cylinder about the axle from −71 to −12 mm. The hub's
    pinion housing is the bracket's turned end for end, by the same half turn that places the
    hub's gears.

### How it is written

- **Every gear is cut as it is made. No involute, helix or trochoid is written down.**
  - An external gear is its blank less the tooth spaces a straight-sided helical rack leaves as
    it rolls on the pitch circle: a hob's generating motion, in the generating-sweep class. The
    rack's tip corners are rounded `0.25 mn`, as a hob's are, so each root fillet is the trochoid
    of a round. `solventc` reads each space's envelope in closed form, the tip round as the
    ellipse it is square to the axis, and carries it up its helix exactly.
    `gears.Helical` takes the tooth count, normal module, pressure angle, helix angle and hand,
    the stub proportions, the backlash, a bore, a hub and the edge chamfer.
  - The ring is an annulus less a left-handed shaper of its own count whose teeth are 0.04 mm
    thicker (the backlash). That is how a gear shaper cuts an internal helical gear.
  - Each blank's rim is chamfered before its teeth are cut, so every tooth end is broken, as on
    a real hobbed gear.
- **The three planets are one figure placed three times.** `Helical` is closed (given only
  numbers), so its solid is evaluated once. Because `(Zs + Zr)/3` is whole, a third of a turn
  is 10 sun pitches and 5 planet pitches. Every planet therefore meets the sun and the ring in
  the same phase, and each is the first one *moved*, not turned.
- **One hypoid pair, placed by what it does, standing twice.**
  - The pair is mated by its members: its frame's origin on the bracket's axis, its gear's
    axis along it, its pinion's axis square to `flip` and running back, the pinion's mean point
    in the shaft's plane. `solventc` lays it out alone, places it whole and carries it.
  - The rear pair is the bracket's pair turned half a turn about `flip`: the normal to the line
    of centres, through its middle, in the shaft's plane. That turn takes the bracket's axis to
    the axle's and the shaft's axis to itself. So the two pinions are coaxial by construction,
    and the pair is laid out and cut once.
- **The frame is made of revolutions.** The case, the pinion housings, the chainstay, the
  dropout case, the hub and the spindle are each a region bounded by cylinders about one axis
  and two ordinates along it, which the kernel builds exactly. The frame is their union less
  their bores. It is rounded where each pinion housing leaves its case (two cylinders on skew
  axes) and where the chainstay leaves each housing.
- **Claims.**
  - `dims.sv` judges the design table: tooth-count arithmetic, the assembly condition, no
    undercut, the contact and overlap ratios, the stresses and the wind-up.
  - `drive.sv` claims that the hub's pinion is coaxial with the bracket's, and the running
    clearances.

## What solventc says

`solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv` (v0.1.18, four cores) solves the drive, the mated pair included,
in about 10 seconds:

```
shaft_drive/drive.sv: solved
  1073 params, 1073 equations, structural rank 1073; DOF 0; 31 components: …
  parts: 35 instances of 16 definitions
```

It then judges seven clearance claims on the built solids, each of which holds:

| claim | measured |
|---|---|
| `shaft clear(3mm) frame`: the shaft runs free inside the chainstay | 5.000 mm |
| `box.carrier clear(0.5mm) frame` | 1.500 mm |
| `bb_gear clear(0.5mm) frame`: the bracket's hypoid gear in its case | 2.561 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) frame`: the hub through the dropout's case | 1.000 mm |
| `hub_gear clear(0.5mm) frame` | 2.256 mm |
| `box.turns.sun clear(0.5mm) bb_pinion`: the pinion's toe clear of the sun's sleeve | 11.76 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) hub_pinion` | 7.209 mm |

The claim that the hub's pinion is on the shaft's axis is a theorem. The face mill's balance
finds a pressure shift of 8.85° and a margin of 0.677 (`--where pair.pressure_shift`). The
whole run, with five pictures, takes 3 min 10 s; on v0.1.17 it took 10 min 46 s.

What the kernel builds exactly, as a B-rep:
- the helical gears: the sun, the ring, and a planet at its own origin (`gears.sv`'s preview);
- both hypoid pinions;
- the carrier and the pins;
- the frame, with its three rounds;
- the hub, the spindle, the shaft and the cranks.

What is drawn only from its material field: the two hypoid gears (11 below), and the planets
where the planetary seats them (12 below).

`solventc shaft_drive/planetary.sv` builds the planetary on its own in about 90 s, and
`solventc shaft_drive/dims.sv` judges the design table's 10 claims, all theorems.

## What solventc lacks

The findings this drawing first hit on v0.1.17 are filed as
[#904](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/issues/904). Each was tried again on v0.1.18.

### Fixed in v0.1.18, and used here

- **A part mated by what it made** (A5). The pair is now placed by relations on its members, as
  above. It solves in seconds. On v0.1.17 the same mate ran 6 minutes into a conflict, and the
  drawing held the pair's frame at an attitude copied out of its own solve.
- **The pair at another scale** (A3). At 0.625 every member builds exactly in the pair's own
  frame. Both pinions also build exactly in the bicycle (but see 11).
- **A rack with rounded tips** (A6). It is generated in closed form; on v0.1.17 it was a fitted
  sheet 17 µm and 4.3° off its contacts. The gears use it.
- **A round between cylinders on skew axes** (A8). It builds: the frame's round area goes from
  953 to 1963 mm². The case-to-housing round is drawn.
- **A gear's mesh refused for orientation** (A7). A polygonal tip relief's STL, and a 0.4 mm
  chamfer at both faces, now export.
- **Solids in a `repeat` have names** (A14). `--solid 'box.planet[0].body'` resolves.
- **A field-read body can be written** (A15). Without OCCT, `--stl-backend mesh` writes it, and
  a refused `--stl` says so. `--stl-backend refine` still refuses a body built on a sweep: "a
  body's stock may not contain a continuous sweep".
- **`--json`** (A12). It takes 10 s on `drive.sv --no-check`, where it ran past 10 minutes.
- **`construction` on a solid written with a set literal** (B2). It is accepted.
- **Other tooth counts** (A1, in part). `HypoidPair` now solves at 12/24 and at 16/32 (1.5 mm),
  and its pinions build. Its gears are drawn from their fields at those counts.
- **A claim over a gear train** (A11). It now warns W117 when it would hold a member still, and
  the primer gives the idiom: each frame's value over one parameter. `mesh.sv` uses it:
  - In the carrier's frame the sun, a planet and the ring turn at their ratios over a whole sun
    pitch, 37 poses.
  - The sun stays clear of the planet by 0.017 mm, the backlash: the claim holds.
  - The planet against the ring measures 0.0086 mm, but the claim is **undecided**: the faceting
    leaves ±0.014 mm of uncertainty, more than the gap it is asked to see (16 below).
  - The run takes about 8 minutes. On v0.1.17 the claim held the planet still and was refuted
    at −1.46 mm.

### Still open on v0.1.18

1. **A spiral bevel with no offset does not solve.** With `--set offset=0mm`, `hypoid.sv` stops at
   "a bound does not hold: `pair.balance.pinion_outer regular(>= 0, …)`". The pinion's outer
   flank folds at every shift the balance can reach. That is now said, where v0.1.17 said only
   that the cut folded.
2. **A tooth's end cannot be broken after it is cut.** `body.blank.near chamfer(d: 0.3mm) body`
   over a hobbed gear is still E080: "an edge cut off by a face neither a plane, a cylinder, a
   cone nor a sphere". The gears chamfer the blank's rim before cutting, as a shop does.
3. **No tooth contact analysis for parallel-axis gears.**
   - `meshing(…)`, ease-off and transmission error exist only for flanks that are envelopes in
     space.
   - There is no reading of a cylindrical pair's contact ratio, transmission error, contact
     pattern or mesh stiffness, and no lead or profile modification.
   - `dims.sv` writes the contact and overlap ratios out by hand from closed forms.
4. **No gear rating.** Root and contact stress (ISO 6336 / AGMA 2001), planet load sharing and the
   shaft's torsional compliance are closed forms in the design table.
5. **No gear vocabulary.**
   - `gears.Helical` re-derives the textbook by hand and takes 14 formals with sentinel zeros.
   - There are no default formals.
   - There is no `meshes` word to set a mate's centre distance, ratio and phase.
   - There is no train to give every frame its ratio (#904 C2, C3, C5).
6. **A `Cylinder` and an ordinate inside a set need a drawn line; an `axis` is refused** (B1).
   `std.sets.Cylinder(std.y, r: …)` is still "`distance` does not relate a point to an axis". The
   drive draws `bb_line` and `axle_line` for this alone.
7. **A value's expression cannot continue across lines** (B3), even inside brackets.
8. **`l0 coincident std.side.origin` in a component** is still E109 where `fix((0mm, 0mm)) l0` is
   accepted (B4).
9. **No collinearity of two lines, even as a claim** (B5), and **no `side:` on a tangency at a
   line's end** (B6).
10. **A `Through` cutter** cut from a blank later cut by a generated sweep is still "a through
    extent needs every material source bounded" (B7). The bores are bounded prisms.
11. **A hypoid gear standing in the bicycle's frame is not built.**
    - The pair's gear builds exactly in the pair's own frame.
    - Mated into the bicycle, its cutting tool's boundary is refused: "a piece of
      `pair.gear_space.outer_crownbase` (a plane) touches `pair.gear_space.neighborbase` (a
      plane) … along no face of either". Its STL is written by `--stl-backend mesh` instead.
    - The pinions, which also failed this way on v0.1.17 (A4), now build.
12. **A helical gear seated off its own origin fails its export's field check.**
    - A planet seated on its pin, in the planetary or the drive, gives "the exported surface
      disagrees with the material field at 5 of 1970 probes; nothing was written".
    - The same binding at its own origin, `gears.sv`'s preview, exports. That is what the CI
      writes.
13. **`--where` through an instance.** A point of a plane in a turned frame prints the plane's
    x and y, not where it stands; a group alias (`--where pair.pinion_member.foot`) still names
    nothing.
14. **A geometric claim that holds prints nothing**, where a claim over values prints
    `— theorem`.
15. **A part with an unknown formal is not closed.** `HypoidPair` finds its pressure shift, so a
    second instance would be laid out and cut again. The rear pair is the first one's solids
    read at a half turn.

16. **A clearance claim cannot resolve a gear's backlash.** `ring_at clear(0.005mm) planet_at` over
    the roll is undecided at every pose: "disjointness is within geometric uncertainty". It
    measured 0.0086 mm, in an interval from −0.0056 to 0.0228 mm, so flanks closer than about
    0.014 mm cannot be told apart from touching.

### Not drawn

Bearings, bushes, seals and circlips (each is a seat in the frame or a running clearance), the
spindle's and pinions' splines, threads (the pedal eye is at 9/16" plain), the left chainstay,
the seat stays and the wheel. The library has no machine elements: a spline is written out by
hand as in `hypoid_drive/drive.sv`, and a bearing is a ring.
