# A shaft-drive bicycle transmission

A chainless drive for a bicycle. The shaft is sealed inside the drive-side chainstay. A step-up
gearbox sits inside an oversized bottom bracket and a step-down gearbox inside the rear dropout.
It is written in Solvent and checked with the **released `solventc` v0.1.17**. It is also a
benchmark: a hard target for the language and its kernel. [What solventc lacks](#what-solventc-lacks)
lists every place where this drawing hit a limit, and what was done about each one.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `dims.sv` | the design table: duty, tooth counts, ratios, stresses and wind-up, with the claims that check them |
| `gears.sv` | `Helical` (an external helical gear generated by a helical rack) and `Ring` (an internal helical gear shaped by an external one) |
| `planetary.sv` | the bottom bracket's helical planetary: sun with its sleeve, three planets on pins, the shaped ring and the carrier |
| `drive.sv` | the assembly: frame datums, the hypoid pairs, the planetary, the shaft, the frame (case, pinion housings, chainstay, dropout case), the hub and the spindle |

```
solventc shaft_drive/dims.sv                        # the design table, every claim judged
solventc shaft_drive/planetary.sv --png planetary.png --view top --hide box.carrier
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv                       # solve, diagnose, judge the clearances
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png drive.png --view 300,25
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png cut.png --view 300,35 --section 'y=-40mm±60mm'
```

Run these from `rust/examples/`. The drive imports `spiral_bevel/hypoid.sv`, and modules are
looked up from the opened file's directory and its ancestors.

## The design

**Why the shaft spins fast.** A drive shaft is a torsion spring between the cranks and the wheel.
Under each pedal stroke it winds up, then gives the energy back late and out of phase. That
springiness is what makes shaft bikes feel soft. The wind-up is `T·L/(G·J)`, and the torque the
shaft carries is the crank torque divided by how much faster than the cranks the shaft turns.

So this drive steps the speed up by `k = 6` before the shaft and back down by 2 at the hub. The
shaft then carries a sixth of the crank torque and winds up a sixth as far. That angle is divided
by 6 again on its way back to the crank. The rider feels a spring `k² = 36` times stiffer than the
same shaft driven 1:1. The 16 × 1.5 mm steel shaft winds up 0.42° at the cranks in a 180 N·m
sprint, against 15.3° if it were driven directly (`dims.sv`: `wind`, `wind_direct`).

**The train.**

| stage | where | gears | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | inside the bottom bracket | helical planetary: spindle → carrier, ring held in the shell, sun out. Sun 30 (right hand), three planets 15 (left), ring 60 (left); normal module 1 mm, 20° normal pressure angle, 20° helix, 14 mm face | × 3 |
| 2 | inside the bottom bracket | hypoid gear 48 on the sun's sleeve drives the hypoid pinion 24 on the shaft; mean module 1.25 mm, 25° spiral, 15.6 mm offset | × 2 |
| 3 | inside the rear dropout | the same hypoid pair turned end for end: the shaft's pinion 24 drives the hub's gear 48 | ÷ 2 |

- **Overall:** the wheel turns 3 times as fast as the cranks, like a 48 × 16 single speed. At
  90 rpm that is 35 km/h on a 700 × 35C tyre. The shaft turns at 540 rpm and carries 8.3 N·m
  cruising (50 N·m at the crank) or 30 N·m sprinting.
- **Quiet and efficient.** Every gear is helical or spiral. Every mesh has more than one tooth
  pair across the face (overlap ratio 1.52) as well as round the profile (transverse contact
  ratio 1.19 sun–planet, 1.36 planet–ring). The hypoids use the library pair's balanced
  face-mill settings. In the planetary, a planet's axial thrust from the sun and from the ring
  cancel, so the planets run free of axial load.
- **Stresses.** These are estimates, to be checked to ISO 6336 before tooling. At the sprint
  peak the planet's Lewis root stress is 289 MPa, against 450 MPa allowable for case-hardened
  18CrNiMo7-6. The shaft's shear is 66 MPa and the spindle's 151 MPa. Each is a claim in
  `dims.sv`.
- **Packaging.**
  - The bottom bracket is an 82 mm gearbox shell, 123 mm face to face. Its right end closes
    round the right-hand spindle bearing, outboard of the hypoid pinion. The pinion's housing
    is a 56 mm cylinder about the shaft's axis, round a 46.8 mm pinion, that runs back into
    the 30 × 2 mm chainstay.
  - The shaft lies in a plane 40 mm out from the frame's mid-plane on the drive side. At the hub
    that clears the spokes of a flange at −11 mm.
  - The dropout's gear case is an 80 mm cylinder about the axle from −71 to −12 mm. The hub's
    pinion housing is the bracket's turned end for end, by the same half turn that places the
    hub's gears.

### How it is written

- **Every gear is cut as it is made. No involute, helix or trochoid is written down.**
  - An external gear is its blank less the tooth spaces a straight-sided helical rack leaves as
    it rolls on the pitch circle: a hob's generating motion, in the generating-sweep class.
    `solventc` reads each space's envelope in closed form and carries it up its helix exactly.
    `gears.Helical` takes the tooth count, normal module, pressure angle, helix angle and hand,
    the stub proportions, the backlash, a bore, a hub and the edge chamfer.
  - The ring is an annulus less a left-handed shaper of its own count whose teeth are 0.04 mm
    thicker (the backlash). That is how a gear shaper cuts an internal helical gear.
  - Each blank's rim is chamfered before its teeth are cut, so every tooth end is broken, as on
    a real hobbed gear.
- **The three planets are one figure placed three times.** `Helical` is closed (given only
  numbers), so its solid is evaluated once. Because `(Zs + Zr)/3` is whole, a third of a turn
  is 10 sun pitches and 5 planet pitches. Every planet therefore meets the sun and the ring in
  the same phase, and each is the first one *moved*, not turned.
- **One hypoid pair, standing twice.** The rear pair is the bracket's pair turned half a turn
  about `flip`: the normal to the line of centres, through its middle, in the shaft's plane.
  That turn takes the bracket's axis to the axle's and the shaft's axis to itself. So the two
  pinions are coaxial by construction, and the pair is laid out and cut once.
- **The frame is made of revolutions.** The case, the pinion housings, the chainstay, the
  dropout case, the hub and the spindle are each a region bounded by cylinders about one axis
  and two ordinates along it, which the kernel builds exactly. The frame is their union less
  their bores, with a round where the chainstay leaves each pinion housing.
- **Claims.**
  - `dims.sv` judges the design table: tooth-count arithmetic, the assembly condition, no
    undercut, the contact and overlap ratios, the stresses and the wind-up.
  - `drive.sv` claims the pair stands where it should (its gear on the bracket's axis, the
    pinion in the shaft's plane), that the hub's pinion is coaxial with the bracket's, and the
    running clearances.

## What solventc says

`solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv` (v0.1.17, four cores) solves the drive in about a second:

```
shaft_drive/drive.sv: solved
  1082 params, 1082 equations, structural rank 1082; DOF 0; 33 components: …
  parts: 33 instances of 17 definitions
```

It then judges seven clearance claims on the built solids, each of which holds:

| claim | measured |
|---|---|
| `shaft clear(3mm) frame`: the shaft runs free inside the chainstay | 5.000 mm |
| `box.carrier clear(0.5mm) frame` | 1.500 mm |
| `bb_gear clear(0.5mm) frame`: the bracket's hypoid gear in its case | 2.561 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) frame`: the hub through the dropout's case | 1.000 mm |
| `hub_gear clear(0.5mm) frame` | 2.256 mm |
| `box.turns.sun clear(0.5mm) bb_pinion`: the pinion's toe clear of the sun's sleeve | 11.76 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) hub_pinion` | 7.298 mm |

The five placement claims (the pair on the bracket's axis and in the shaft's plane, the hub's
pinion on the shaft's axis) are theorems. The run, with five pictures, takes about 11 minutes.
Most of that is the four hypoid members, which are read from their fields: 6–25 s to build
each, and 15–60 s for each claim against them.

What the kernel builds exactly, as a B-rep:
- every helical gear: the sun, the planets and the ring;
- the carrier and the pins;
- the frame, with its two coaxial rounds;
- the hub, the spindle, the shaft and the cranks.

What is drawn only from its material field: the four hypoid members (see 2 and 3 below).

`solventc shaft_drive/planetary.sv` builds the planetary on its own in 30 s, and
`solventc shaft_drive/dims.sv` judges the design table's 10 claims, all theorems.

## What solventc lacks

Ranked by what each cost this design.  Each says what was tried, what `solventc` v0.1.17 said,
and what the drawing does instead.

### Gears

1. **The spiral bevel/hypoid pair cannot be re-targeted.**
   - `spiral_bevel/hypoid.sv`'s `HypoidPair` solves only near its own 24/48 teeth. At 12/24,
     13/27 and 16/32 (1.5 mm) it stops at its iteration limit with conflicts inside `GearCone`
     and `ToothTrace`.
   - At its own counts with no offset, a plain spiral bevel, the pinion's cut is refused (the
     generated surface's area factor is −8.2e−4, a fold), and its field mesh fails ("the feature
     curves could not be protected").
   - So the drive could not choose its bevel ratio or tooth counts. Both stages use the
     library's 24/48 hypoid.
   - *Needed:* a layout that continues from a solved pair to the numbers asked for, rather than
     solving from scratch out of seeds that do not track the counts.
2. **The pair is not scale-invariant when built.**
   - At its own 2 mm mean module the pair builds exactly, but its gear is 107 mm across, which
     no bottom bracket holds. Every length was therefore scaled by 0.625: 1.25 mm, a 67 mm gear.
   - At that scale the layout solves and the gear builds, but the pinion's generating cut is
     refused: "a station's contact curve ends inside the blank: the stations reach walk lengths
     −2.232 to 16.387 where the blank holds contacts from −2.438 to 15.637".
   - At 0.75 the cut is refused too: "the fitted sheet leaves its contacts: 0.099 mm and 89.9
     degrees".
   - The pinions are therefore drawn from their material fields and cannot be exported as STEP.
   - *Needed:* the generating class's tolerances in the part's own scale.
3. **Exactness depends on where a part stands.**
   - Standing in the pair's own frame, the scaled gear builds exactly.
   - Placed in the bicycle, in a frame turned to it, the same gear falls back to its field
     ("`pair.gear.cut.removal` is a continuous motion sweep, read by its field").
4. **A rack with a tip radius leaves the exact class.**
   - Every hob has rounded tips; they cut the root fillet. A rack tooth whose tip corners are
     rounded (`-> round(r: 0.25 * mn)`) is generated as a *fitted* sheet: "within 1.73e-2 mm of
     the 758 withheld contacts …, normals within 4.31 degrees". That is not gear-grade.
   - A straight-sided rack stays in the closed form, an involute envelope carried up its helix.
     The gears here are cut by sharp-cornered racks, so their roots are the trochoids of sharp
     corners.
   - A polygonal tip relief, the corners chamfered, builds exactly, but its STL is refused: "the
     mesh has 1 triangles facing against their surfaces".
5. **A tooth's end cannot be broken after it is cut.**
   - `body.blank.near chamfer(d: 0.3mm) body` over a hobbed gear is E080: "an edge cut off by a
     face neither a plane, a cylinder, a cone nor a sphere: no exact construction is made of it
     yet". The face is the involute helicoid.
   - The gears chamfer the blank's rim before cutting, as a shop does. That breaks the tips but
     not the flanks' ends.
   - That path is fragile too. A 0.4 mm chamfer at both faces gives "15 triangles facing against
     their surfaces", one per tooth, and the STL and STEP are refused. A 0.3 mm chamfer at both
     faces, or 0.4 mm at one face, builds.
6. **No tooth contact analysis for parallel-axis gears.**
   - `meshing(…)`, ease-off and transmission error exist for flanks that are envelopes in space,
     the bevel family.
   - A cylindrical gear's flank is a planar envelope extruded or screwed, so it has no word for:
     contact ratio, transmission error, contact pattern, mesh stiffness, or lead and profile
     modifications (crowning, tip relief, end relief).
   - The contact and overlap ratios that say this train runs quietly are written out by hand in
     `dims.sv` from closed forms, not read from the gears drawn.
7. **No gear rating.**
   - The root stresses are Lewis estimates in the design table.
   - Scenarios analyse frames and solids, but nothing reads a mesh's contact (Hertz) stress, a
     rating (ISO 6336 / AGMA 2001), or a mesh's load sharing among three planets.
   - The shaft's torsional wind-up, the reason for the design, is a closed form too.
   - A mesh can be checked at rest but not as it rolls.
     - At rest, `claim box.sun.body clear(0.005mm) box.planet[0].body` holds, measured 0.019 mm
       (the backlash, ±0.011 mm from the faceting).
     - Rolling: with the sun under a frame that `spins(1)` and the planet under one that
       `spins(-Zs / Zp)` about its pin, `claim over sun_turn in (0deg, 12deg) { sun_at clear(…)
       planet_at }` is refuted at 6°, −1.46 mm. A sweep moves the solids under the frame it names,
       so the planet appears to stand still while the sun turns half a pitch into it.
     - Nothing says "these frames turn together at their ratios" for a claim to sweep a train.
     - The probe took 26 minutes.

### Placing parts

8. **A part with a delicate internal solve cannot be mated by its own geometry.**
   - The pair should be placed by what its function says: its gear's axis on the bracket's, its
     pinion's axis along the chainstay, in the shaft's plane. Five rows on its six freedoms,
     with `pair.std.origin` read to free its frame.
   - Stated so, the solve ran 6 minutes and stopped in conflict inside the pair's layout. It did
     the same when started from a frame within a few degrees of the answer.
   - The pair solves at once in a frame held by numbers. So `drive.sv` holds `place` at the
     attitude read from the pair's own solve, and *claims* the five relations (each holds).
     Those numbers would go stale if the pair changed.
   - *Needed:* a part solved in its own frame first and then placed as a rigid body: a
     two-phase solve, or rigid reduction of an instance whose interior its frame does not
     reach.
9. **Reading an instance's members does not free its frame; only reading its `std` does.**
   Relations on `pair.gear_member.ax` alone leave the pair in the world frame, over-constrained.
   The primer says so, but the report blames the pair's own rows.
10. **A solid that is the source of an `at` copy is dropped from the picture without a word.**
    `hub_gear := bb_gear at(under: flip) 180deg` silently removed `bb_gear` from every PNG. Both
    are now copies of one construction solid.
11. **A part with an unknown formal is not closed.** `HypoidPair` leaves its face mill's settings
    to the solve, so two instances would be laid out and cut twice. The rear pair is the front
    pair's solids read at a half turn (`at(under: flip) 180deg`), which also says the design
    intent.

### The kernel

12. **A round between cylinders on skew axes fails.** The round where the pinion's housing (a
    cylinder about the shaft's axis) leaves the bracket's case (a cylinder about the bracket's
    axis), `frame.shell.boss round(r: 4mm) frame.shell.case_outer`, leaves the frame unbuilt:
    "an operand could not be evaluated at this approximation". The coaxial rounds, where the
    chainstay leaves each housing, build. The case-to-housing round is left out.
13. **The frame's exact build is sensitive to sizes.**
    - With the pinion's housing 48 mm across, the frame without rounds was built only from its
      facets: "face 13 (plane): a face's loops do not close its triangulation".
    - At 56 mm across, which the 46.8 mm pinion needs anyway, it builds exactly.
14. **Run time.** The whole drive takes 10 min 46 s on four cores: solve, seven clearance claims
    and five pictures. A clearance claim against a hypoid member takes 15–60 s, because each
    member is read from its field.

### The language

15. **A `Cylinder` and an ordinate inside a set need a drawn line; an `axis` is refused.**
    `std.sets.Cylinder(std.y, r: …)` and `std.origin distance(in: (a, b), along: std.y) q` in a
    set's body are E040: "`distance` does not relate a point to an axis". The same ordinate
    outside a set is accepted. The drive draws `bb_line` and `axle_line` along its two axes for
    this alone.
16. **`construction` cannot prefix a solid written with a set literal:** `construction x :=
    solid({ q | … })` is E001, "geometry modifiers apply to declarations, chains, or component
    instances". The set is named first.
17. **A value's expression cannot continue across lines**, even inside brackets (`eps := (a +
    b` newline `- c) / d` is "a statement starts with a word"). A claim's can.
18. **Inconsistent incidence to a datum in a component.** `l0 coincident std.side.origin` is
    E109 ("past what its own unknowns take") where `fix((0mm, 0mm)) l0` and `o coincident
    std.top.origin` are accepted.
19. **No collinearity of two lines**, even as a claim: `claim a coincident b` over two lines is
    "`coincident` does not relate a line to a line". The drive claims each pinion's foot on the
    other's axis instead.
20. **A tangency at a line's end takes no `side:`.** `lower tangent(at: p1) big` picks the
    external tangent by its seed alone, where a word should say it.
21. **`--json` ran past 10 minutes** on `drive.sv --no-check`, where the text report takes 6 s.
22. **A part seated with its front plane on the world's reads its orientations off the wrong
    plane.**
    - Turned half a turn about the spindle and seated at the origin, the left crank's front lies
      on the world's.
    - `solventc` warns W113 from inside `std` (`std:55:3: v54 lies on std.front …`), naming no
      line of the document.
    - The crank's `ccw(std.origin, c, d)` then breaks ("orientation broken"), though the same
      figure holds it on the right.
    - Seating both cranks off the world's front plane, at the spindle's ends, cures it.

### Not drawn

Bearings, bushes, seals and circlips (each is a seat in the frame or a running clearance), the
spindle's and pinions' splines, threads (the pedal eye is at 9/16" plain), the left chainstay,
the seat stays and the wheel. The library has no machine elements: a spline is written out by
hand as in `hypoid_drive/drive.sv`, and a bearing is a ring.
