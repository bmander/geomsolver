# A shaft-drive bicycle

A chainless road bicycle's frame and drive, in Solvent.
- **The frame:** a 56 cm road frame with a centred bottom bracket and a rear wheel on a 142 mm
  thru-axle.
- **The drive:**
  - The shaft runs sealed inside the drive-side chainstay. It splays outward toward the hub as a
    chainstay does, so it meets neither axle square.
  - A step-up gearbox sits inside the bottom bracket's shell, and a step-down gearbox inside the
    drive-side dropout.
  - Every shaft turns in ball bearings, each held by a retaining ring in its groove.

It is checked with the **released `solventc` v0.1.19**. It is also a benchmark: a hard target for
the language and its kernel. Its findings on v0.1.17 were filed as
[#904](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/issues/904), and
[What solventc lacks](#what-solventc-lacks) says which v0.1.18 and v0.1.19 fixed, what is still open,
and what the frame and hardware found.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `dims.sv` | the design table: duty, tooth counts, ratios, stresses and wind-up, the frame's geometry, the bearings' and rings' sizes, with the claims that check them |
| `frame.sv` | `Frame`: the main triangle, the stays, the non-drive chainstay and dropout, as hollow tubes between the frame's nodes |
| `gears.sv` | `Helical` (an external helical gear generated by a helical rack) and `Ring` (an internal helical gear shaped by an external one) |
| `planetary.sv` | the bottom bracket's helical planetary: sun with its sleeve, three planets on pins, the shaped ring and the carrier |
| `mesh.sv` | the planetary's meshes checked as they roll: sun, planet and ring turned at their ratios over a sun pitch |
| `drive.sv` | the assembly: the frame's datums, the spiral bevel pairs, the planetary, the shaft, the spindle and cranks, the frame with its drive housings, the bearing cup, the hub and thru-axle, and every bearing and ring on its station |

```
solventc shaft_drive/dims.sv                        # the design table, every claim judged
solventc shaft_drive/mesh.sv                        # the planetary's meshes, rolled over a sun pitch
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv                       # solve, diagnose, judge the clearances
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png bike.png --view 300,20
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png cut.png --view 290,25 --section 'y=-30mm'
solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv --png drive.png --view 300,20 --hide frame,bearing_cup
```

Run these from `rust/examples/`. The drive imports `spiral_bevel/hypoid.sv`, and modules are
looked up from the opened file's directory and its ancestors.

## The design

**Why the shaft spins fast.** A drive shaft is a torsion spring between the cranks and the wheel.
Under each pedal stroke it winds up, then gives the energy back late and out of phase. That
springiness is what makes shaft bikes feel soft. The wind-up is `T·L/(G·J)`, and the torque the
shaft carries is the crank torque divided by how much faster than the cranks the shaft turns.

So this drive steps the speed up by `k = 6` before the shaft and back down by 2 at the hub. The
shaft then carries a sixth of the crank torque and winds up a sixth as far. That angle is divided
by 6 again on its way back to the crank. The rider feels a spring `k² = 36` times stiffer than the
same shaft driven 1:1. The 17 × 1.5 mm steel shaft winds up 0.35° at the cranks in a 180 N·m
sprint, against 12.5° if it were driven directly (`dims.sv`: `wind`, `wind_direct`).

**The train.**

| stage | where | gears | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | inside the bottom bracket | helical planetary: spindle → carrier, ring held in the shell, sun out. Sun 30 (right hand), three planets 15 (left), ring 60 (left); normal module 1 mm, 20° normal pressure angle, 20° helix, 14 mm face | × 3 |
| 2 | inside the bottom bracket | spiral bevel gear 48 on the sun's sleeve drives the spiral bevel pinion 24 on the shaft; mean module 1.25 mm, 25° spiral, **shafts at 92°** | × 2 |
| 3 | inside the drive-side dropout | the same pair turned end for end: the shaft's pinion 24 drives the hub's gear 48, its shafts at 92° too | ÷ 2 |

- **Overall:** the wheel turns 3 times as fast as the cranks, like a 48 × 16 single speed. At
  90 rpm that is 35 km/h on a 700 × 35C tyre. The shaft turns at 540 rpm and carries 8.3 N·m
  cruising (50 N·m at the crank) or 30 N·m sprinting.
- **Quiet and efficient.**
  - Every gear is helical or spiral.
  - Every mesh has more than one tooth pair across the face (overlap ratio 1.52) as well as round
    the profile (transverse contact ratio 1.19 sun–planet, 1.36 planet–ring).
  - The bevels are spiral bevels, not hypoids: their axes meet, so they lose none of their work
    to the lengthwise sliding a hypoid's offset brings. Their face mill's pressure shift is found
    where the pinion's two flanks stand equally far from undercut.
  - In the planetary, a planet's axial thrust from the sun and from the ring cancel, so the
    planets run free of axial load.
- **Stresses.** These are estimates, to be checked to ISO 6336 before tooling. At the sprint
  peak the planet's Lewis root stress is 289 MPa, against 450 MPa allowable for case-hardened
  18CrNiMo7-6. The shaft's shear is 58 MPa and the spindle's 151 MPa. Each is a claim in
  `dims.sv`.

**The frame.** A 56 cm road frame in 7005 aluminium, drawn from the numbers a frame is specified
by:

| | |
|---|---|
| stack, reach | 575, 385 mm |
| seat tube | 73.5°, 540 mm (centre to top), 34.9 × 1.65 |
| head tube | 72°, 160 mm, 50 × 3 |
| top tube, down tube | 31.8 × 1.5, 44 × 1.5 |
| chainstay, BB drop | 430 mm, 70 mm |
| seat stays | 16 × 1.5, coped to the seat tube's flanks 9 mm either side of its axis, 28 mm under the top tube |
| non-drive chainstay | 22 × 1.5 |
| rear axle | 142 mm over the dropouts, 12 mm thru-axle |

- **The bottom bracket is centred.** It is a 100 mm shell, ±50 mm about the frame's mid-plane,
  82 mm across: the gearbox's case.
  - The ring gear is pressed into its 72 mm bore.
  - Its right end is closed round the right spindle bearing.
  - Its left end takes a pressed bearing cup.
  - The cranks stand ±62 mm from the mid-plane, clear of the pinion's housing.
- **The shaft splays with the chainstay.** It runs from 32 mm out on the drive side at the
  bracket to 45 mm out at the hub. Seen from above it leans 2° outward, so each bevel pair's
  shafts meet at 92°. Seen from the side it lies on the line of centres, the bevels having no
  offset.
  - Its chainstay is 30 × 2 mm.
  - It joins a 56 mm housing about the shaft's axis round each 46.8 mm pinion.
  - It is rounded where it leaves each housing.
- **The drive-side dropout is the rear gearbox's case.** It is an 80 mm cylinder about the axle,
  from the dropout's face at −71 mm to −20 mm, round the hub's gear and pinion. The hub's pinion
  housing is the bracket's turned end for end, by the same half turn that places the hub's gears.
- **The stays** end on the dropouts, 26 mm short of the axle's centre. The non-drive dropout is a
  plate about the axle that they are welded to.

**Bearings and retaining rings.** Deep-groove ball bearings (ISO 15) with their balls, and
retaining rings (DIN 471 on a shaft, DIN 472 in a bore), each in its groove:

| where | bearing | held by |
|---|---|---|
| spindle, right (in the shell's end wall) | 6804, 20 × 32 × 7 | DIN 471 Ø20 inboard |
| spindle, left (in the bearing cup) | 6804 | DIN 471 Ø20 inboard |
| sun's sleeve (in the shell's diaphragm) | 6805, 25 × 37 × 7 | DIN 471 Ø25, its groove cut in the sleeve |
| shaft, behind each pinion (in the housing's web) | 6903, 17 × 30 × 7 | DIN 471 Ø17 behind it |
| hub, each end (on the 12 mm thru-axle) | 6901, 12 × 24 × 6 | DIN 472 Ø24 outboard, in the hub's bore |

Not drawn: the cages and seals, the planets' and the spindle's bushes, the splines, and threads
(the pedal eyes are plain 9/16").

### How it is written

- **Every gear is cut as it is made. No involute, helix or trochoid is written down.**
  - An external gear is its blank less the tooth spaces a straight-sided helical rack leaves as
    it rolls on the pitch circle: a hob's generating motion, in the generating-sweep class. The
    rack's tip corners are rounded `0.25 mn`, as a hob's are, so each root fillet is the trochoid
    of a round. `solventc` reads each space's envelope in closed form, the tip round as the
    ellipse it is square to the axis, and carries it up its helix exactly.
    `gears.Helical` takes the tooth count, normal module, pressure angle, helix angle and hand,
    the stub proportions, the backlash, a bore, a hub and the edge chamfer.
  - The ring is an annulus less a left-handed shaper of its own count whose teeth are 0.04 mm
    thicker (the backlash). That is how a gear shaper cuts an internal helical gear.
  - Each blank's rim is chamfered before its teeth are cut, so every tooth end is broken, as on
    a real hobbed gear.
  - The spiral bevels are `spiral_bevel/hypoid.sv`'s pair with no offset, at 92°, cut by its
    generating crown.
- **The three planets are one figure placed three times.** `Helical` is closed (given only
  numbers), so its solid is evaluated once. Because `(Zs + Zr)/3` is whole, a third of a turn
  is 10 sun pitches and 5 planet pitches. Every planet therefore meets the sun and the ring in
  the same phase, and each is the first one *moved*, not turned.
- **One bevel pair, placed by what it does, standing twice.**
  - The pair is mated by its members: its frame's origin on the bracket's axis, its gear's axis
    along it, its pinion's axis square to `flip` and running back, the pinion's mean point 32 mm
    out. `solventc` lays it out alone, places it whole and carries it.
  - `flip` is the line square to both axles and to the shaft, through the shaft's point over the
    middle of the line of centres.
  - The rear pair is the bracket's turned half a turn about `flip`. That turn takes the
    bracket's axis to a line along the axle through its centre, and the shaft to itself. So the
    two pinions are coaxial by construction, whatever the splay, and the pair is laid out and
    cut once.
- **The frame is tubes about lines, coped where they meet.**
  - `frame.Frame` places the frame's nodes from stack, reach and the tube angles and lengths, in
    the side view and either side of it.
  - Each tube is the region between two cylinders about the line between its nodes, cut off by
    ordinates along it, and carries a `plug`: the same cylinder as a bar.
  - A mitre is one statement, `head.plug cut top.body`: the joining tube cut to the receiving
    tube's outside, a fish-mouth cope, its wall gone from the receiving tube's bore. The top tube
    is coped to the seat and head tubes, the down tube to the head tube, the seat stays to the
    seat tube. The tubes meeting the bottom bracket end inside its shell, whose bore cuts them.
  - The drive's housings are cylinders about the axes they house, unioned with the tubes and
    then bored.
- **Hardware is joined through its ports** (`std.ports`, `std.bearings`, `std.retaining`;
  `docs/ports-plan.md`).
  - Each bearing is seated in a `Station` of the body that carries it: the shell's end wall, the
    bearing cup, the diaphragm, a pinion's web, the hub. A station is where the bearing's mid-plane
    stands on its axis, pointing the way the bearing went in.
  - `OnShaft` (DIN 471, on a shaft) or `InBore` (DIN 472, in a bore) seats the bearing there and
    cuts the room it takes from the station's body. Its ring abuts the bearing's trailing side, and
    the ring's groove is cut in what it is fitted to (`spindle_j := {host: spindle}`).
  - The bearings and rings are the library's catalogue rows (`std.bearings.b6804`,
    `std.retaining.din471_20`), so no bore, groove or ring position is typed in the design. The
    spindle's and shaft's diameters read their bearings' bores.
  - Each 6804, 6903 and 6901, and each ring of a size, is one figure placed twice.
- **Claims.**
  - `dims.sv` judges the design table: tooth-count arithmetic, the assembly condition, no
    undercut, the contact and overlap ratios, the stresses and the wind-up.
  - `drive.sv` claims that the hub's pinion is coaxial with the bracket's, and ten running
    clearances: shaft, carrier, gears, hub and pinions, the spindle's bearing and the cranks.

## What solventc says

`solventc shaft_drive/drive.sv` (v0.1.19, four cores) solves the bicycle, the mated bevel pair
included, in about 15 seconds:

```
shaft_drive/drive.sv: solved
  2008 params, 2008 equations, structural rank 2008; DOF 0; 152 components: …
  parts: 106 instances of 26 definitions
```

It then judges ten clearance claims on the built solids, each of which holds:

| claim | measured |
|---|---|
| `shaft clear(3mm) frame`: the shaft runs free inside the chainstay | 4.500 mm |
| `box.carrier clear(0.5mm) frame` | 4.000 mm |
| `bb_gear clear(0.5mm) frame`: the bracket's bevel gear in the shell | 2.081 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) frame`: the hub through the dropout's case and past the stays | 1.000 mm |
| `hub_gear clear(0.5mm) frame` | 3.124 mm |
| `box.turns.sun clear(0.5mm) bb_pinion`: the pinion's toe clear of the sun's sleeve | 14.03 mm |
| `right_bb.bearing.outer_ring clear(0.5mm) bb_pinion`: and of the spindle's right bearing | 9.537 mm |
| `hub clear(0.5mm) hub_pinion` | 10.54 mm |
| `right_crank.body clear(2mm) frame`: the crank past the pinion's housing | 2.417 mm |
| `left_crank.body clear(2mm) frame` | 17.70 mm |

The claims earned their keep. Their first run refuted three of them, and each refutation named the
two faces and the point:
- the carrier 0.5 mm from the diaphragm;
- the left seat stay running into the hub's end, because the stays ended at the axle's centre;
- the right crank 2.7 mm into the pinion's housing.

After those were fixed, a fourth was refuted: the right spoke flange 1 mm into the hub's pinion
housing. Each fix is a number in `dims.sv`:
- the planetary 3 mm to the left;
- the stays ending 26 mm short of the axle;
- the cranks at ±62 mm;
- the pinion housings stopping short of the axles;
- the flange at −14 mm.

The claim that the hub's pinion is on the shaft's axis is a theorem. The face mill's balance
finds a pressure shift of −2.05° and a margin of 0.383 (`--where pair.pressure_shift`). The whole
run, with four pictures and two STLs, takes 3 min 24 s.

Everything is built exactly by the kernel, as a B-rep:
- the helical gears;
- both spiral bevel gears and both pinions, in the bicycle's frame;
- the carrier and the pins;
- the frame, coped at every joint, with its two rounds, and the bearing cup;
- the hub, the thru-axle, the spindle, the shaft and the cranks;
- every bearing's rings and balls, and every retaining ring.

The exception is the planets where they are seated (12 below), which don't export as STL, though
they draw.

`solventc shaft_drive/mesh.sv` rolls the planetary's meshes (below), and `solventc
shaft_drive/dims.sv` judges the design table's 10 claims, all theorems.

## What solventc lacks

The findings this drawing first hit on v0.1.17 are filed as
[#904](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/issues/904). Each was tried again on v0.1.18, and
the frame, the splayed shaft and the hardware found more, 17 to 22. Of those, v0.1.19 fixed
the last four.

### Fixed in v0.1.18, and used here

- **A part mated by what it made** (A5). The bevel pair is placed by relations on its members.
  It solves in seconds. On v0.1.17 the same mate ran 6 minutes into a conflict.
- **A bevel pair at another scale and in a turned frame** (A3, A4). At 0.625, with its shafts at
  92° and mated into the bicycle, every member of the spiral bevel pair builds exactly.
- **A spiral bevel with no offset** (A2). It solves when the pinion's two flanks are tied to
  each other, as `hypoid.sv`'s comment says to for too little offset. With the hypoid's tie
  (pinion inner to gear outer) the pinion's outer flank folds. It warns that `PinionCone` "is
  singular at what it is given" at zero offset, harmlessly.
- **A rack with rounded tips** (A6). It is generated in closed form, and the gears use it.
- **A gear's mesh refused for orientation** (A7). A polygonal tip relief's STL, and a 0.4 mm
  chamfer at both faces, now export.
- **Solids in a `repeat` have names** (A14). `--solid 'box.planet[0].body'` resolves.
- **A field-read body can be written** (A15). Without OCCT, `--stl-backend mesh` writes it.
- **`--json`** (A12). It takes 10 s on `drive.sv --no-check`, where it ran past 10 minutes.
- **`construction` on a solid written with a set literal** (B2). It is accepted.
- **Other tooth counts** (A1, in part). `HypoidPair` solves at 12/24 and 16/32 (1.5 mm), and
  their pinions build. Their gears are drawn from their fields.
- **A claim over a gear train** (A11). W117, and the idiom of each frame's value over one
  parameter. `mesh.sv` uses it:
  - In the carrier's frame the sun, a planet and the ring turn at their ratios over a whole sun
    pitch, 37 poses.
  - The sun stays clear of the planet by 0.017 mm, the backlash: the claim holds.
  - The planet against the ring measures 0.0086 mm, but the claim is **undecided**: the faceting
    leaves ±0.014 mm of uncertainty (16 below).
  - The run takes about 8 minutes.

### Still open on v0.1.18

2. **A tooth's end cannot be broken after it is cut.** `body.blank.near chamfer(d: 0.3mm) body`
   over a hobbed gear is still E080: "an edge cut off by a face neither a plane, a cylinder, a
   cone nor a sphere". The gears chamfer the blank's rim before cutting, as a shop does.
3. **No tooth contact analysis for parallel-axis gears.**
   - `meshing(…)`, ease-off and transmission error exist only for flanks that are envelopes in
     space.
   - There is no reading of a cylindrical pair's contact ratio, transmission error, contact
     pattern or mesh stiffness, and no lead or profile modification.
   - `dims.sv` writes the contact and overlap ratios out by hand from closed forms.
4. **No gear rating.** Root and contact stress (ISO 6336 / AGMA 2001), planet load sharing and the
   shaft's torsional compliance are closed forms in the design table.
5. **No gear vocabulary.**
   - `gears.Helical` re-derives the textbook by hand and takes 14 formals with sentinel zeros.
   - There are no default formals.
   - There is no `meshes` word to set a mate's centre distance, ratio and phase.
   - There is no train to give every frame its ratio (#904 C2, C3, C5).
6. **A `Cylinder` and an ordinate inside a set need a drawn line; an `axis` is refused** (B1).
   The drive draws `bb_line` and `axle_line` for this alone.
7. **A value's expression cannot continue across lines** (B3), even inside brackets.
8. **`l0 coincident std.side.origin` in a component** is still E109 where `fix((0mm, 0mm)) l0` is
   accepted (B4).
9. **No collinearity of two lines, even as a claim** (B5), and **no `side:` on a tangency at a
   line's end** (B6).
10. **A `Through` cutter** cut from a blank later cut by a generated sweep is still "a through
    extent needs every material source bounded" (B7). The bores are bounded prisms.
12. **A helical gear seated off its own origin fails its export's field check.** A planet seated
    on its pin gives "the exported surface disagrees with the material field at 5 of 1970 probes;
    nothing was written". The same binding at its own origin (`gears.sv`'s preview) exports.
13. **`--where` through an instance.** A point of a plane in a turned frame prints the plane's
    x and y; a group alias (`--where pair.pinion_member.foot`) names nothing.
14. **A geometric claim that holds prints nothing**, where a claim over values prints
    `— theorem`.
15. **A part with an unknown formal is not closed.** The pair finds its pressure shift, so a
    second instance would be laid out and cut again. The rear pair is the first one's solids
    read at a half turn.
16. **A clearance claim cannot resolve a gear's backlash.** `ring_at clear(0.005mm) planet_at`
    over the roll is undecided at every pose: "disjointness is within geometric uncertainty".
    It measured 0.0086 mm in an interval from −0.0056 to 0.0228 mm.

### Fixed in v0.1.19, and used here

These four were fixed from this drawing, each with a test that holds the case:

19. **Small balls far from the origin meshed to nothing** ([#915](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/pull/915)).
    The bearings near the hub wrote an STL of no triangles. A seated ball's arc had solved to a
    negative radius, which the CAD recipe wrote signed, and the mesher dropped a sphere's first
    strip across its seam as having no area. Every bearing's balls now write.
20. **A ball in a `ring` was refused** ([#918](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/pull/918)), E023
    for the region's hidden probe and E080 for every copy past the first. A ball written as a
    set is now one ball a copy. (The bearings keep their single ball turned `at` each pitch,
    which reads as well.)
21. **`level` could not name a point** ([#916](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/pull/916)). A
    name the document declares now ends its line, though a relation word shares it.
22. **A union the kernel built was refused as an STL** ([#917](https://github.com/bmander/geomsolver/pull/917)):
    two faces drew one diagonal, and the written mesh had an edge four triangles met at.

### Found by the frame and the hardware (v0.1.18, and 23 on v0.1.19)

17. **A round on skew axes is refused where the housing meets the shell's end.**
    - In the centred shell, the pinion's housing leaves the shell where the shell's end face
      meets its side.
    - `frame.shell.boss round(r: 4mm) frame.shell.case_outer` is refused: "a rolled round: the
      ball of `case_outer…Cylinder` with `boss…Cylinder` is larger than `case_outer…Cylinder`
      can hold". The same round built in the earlier, offset shell (A8).
    - Worse, the whole frame then goes undrawn: "the material field has no boundary yet".
    - The round is left out.
18. **A tube's end is not a dropout.** A frame's stays end on a plate, which `frame.sv` gives a
    plate of its own; tubes about lines have nothing like a dropout or a lug.
    - The stays first ended at the axle's centre and ran into the hub's end, 2.1 mm deep; the
      claim found it.
    - There is no tube end cut to a face, or blended into a plate, short of drawing the end
      with an ordinate.
23. **A weld's fillet is refused at most coped joints** (v0.1.19). The frame's tubes are coped
    (`head.plug cut top.body`), and a 3 mm fillet at each joint, written over the paths in the
    frame's union (`body.top.body round(r: 3mm) body.seat.body`):
    - the top tube onto the seat tube builds, and writes, in 5 s;
    - the top tube onto the head tube is E080, "a stretch of a circle its end does not cut off: no
      exact construction is made of it yet";
    - the down tube onto the head tube is E080, "a rolled round: the fillet of `f.head.body.wall`
      with `f.down.body…Cylinder` runs on smoothly into `f.head.plug…`, but its contact crosses
      their seam in another section than its edge does". The cope leaves the down tube's end on
      the plug's cylinder, which is the head tube's own: one surface, two faces, a seam the round
      cannot cross;
    - all three, drawn, ran past 30 minutes and were stopped;
    - written between the tubes' own solids (`top.body round(r: 3mm) seat.body`) it is a cascade
      of E080 naming internal keys (`f.#1073741939.0…taken`: "the faces a set is on are one
      body's").
    The welds are left out.

### Not drawn

The bearings' cages and seals, the seals and bushes about the spindle and the axle, the spindle's
and pinions' splines, threads (the pedal eye is at 9/16" plain), the fork, the bars, the saddle
and the wheel. The library has no machine elements: a spline would be written out by hand as in
`hypoid_drive/drive.sv`, and the bearings and rings are `std.bearings`' and `std.retaining`'s.
